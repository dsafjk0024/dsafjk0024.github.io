Overview

A manipulator following a trajectory should slow down when something enters its path, and it should do that for objects nobody modelled in advance. This layer takes the depth camera's raw point cloud, computes the distance from the arm's geometry to it with GJK, and scales the commanded velocity by that distance — no proximity sensors on the arm, no obstacle model, no prior segmentation.

Design decisions

  • Distance against the cloud, not against a fitted object. Fitting a shape first means the system only avoids obstacles it can classify; GJK on the points themselves makes an unmodelled object — a hand, a box, a cable — stop the arm the same way.
  • Continuous damping instead of a stop threshold. Scaling velocity by clearance avoids the on/off behaviour that makes an arm lurch and then freeze, which is what matters if a person is working next to it.
  • Same controller in simulation and on hardware. Validated in MuJoCo first and moved to the real xArm6 unchanged, at 30 Hz.

Results

Qualitative. The arm slows as the obstacle closes and stops short of contact, in simulation and on hardware, for obstacles introduced by hand during motion. Clearance at stop, reaction latency and behaviour across approach speeds were not measured — the videos are the evidence as it stands.
