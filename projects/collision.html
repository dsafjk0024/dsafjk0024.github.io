Overview

A distance-based control layer slows the xArm6 as obstacles approach. A RealSense D435i depth camera supplies obstacle point clouds. Robot links and obstacles are approximated by capsules, and GJK computes their minimum distance. Within a configured threshold, that distance scales the motion velocity without replanning the path.

The setup uses a depth camera, with no dedicated proximity sensors on the robot. It does not infer obstacle locations without sensing.

Design decisions

  • Convex geometric approximation. Capsule representations make robot–obstacle distance queries explicit; GJK is applied to those representations.
  • Distance-based damping. Motion slows as clearance decreases inside the threshold, allowing the controller to react each cycle without a new path-planning solution.
  • Simulation-to-real validation. MuJoCo-based system identification supported transfer to the xArm6, where the controller was exercised at 30 Hz.

Results

Qualitative. The arm slows as the obstacle closes and stops short of contact, in simulation and on hardware, for obstacles introduced by hand during motion. Clearance at stop, reaction latency and behaviour across approach speeds were not measured — the videos are the evidence as it stands.
