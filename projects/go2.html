Overview

A latent world model is pretrained in simulation, then planned against on a real quadruped — and the gap between the two is closed by fine-tuning only the dynamics on real-robot data. I am reproducing and deploying this approach, published as SimDist, on a Unitree Go2, and rebuilt the training stack into a modular RL framework so world-model variants can be compared under identical conditions.

Method

The point of the split is that only the dynamics are wrong on the real robot. The encoder, reward and value models learned in simulation stay frozen, so adaptation is a supervised system-identification problem on a handful of real rollouts rather than another round of reinforcement learning on hardware.

What I did

  • Refactored the reference implementation into a modular RL framework, so world-model variants share one config, one training loop and one evaluation path — a change made because otherwise every ablation is also a code difference.
  • Built the proprioceptive observation pipeline over the Unitree SDK — joint positions and velocities, projected gravity — and the deployment loop that plans at 50 Hz.
  • Deployed on the physical Go2 and compared the simulation-only policy against the dynamics-fine-tuned one.

Results so far

Qualitative only — a quantitative evaluation is still to be collected. Transferred straight from simulation the gait is visibly unstable on hardware; after dynamics fine-tuning the same model walks. The videos below are the comparison as it stands, and the numbers that would make it a claim — tracking error, distance before a fall, success across commanded velocities — are the next thing to measure.
