Overview

An end-to-end imitation learning pipeline on a low-cost SO-101 leader–follower arm pair: teleoperate the follower by hand-guiding the leader, record episodes, convert them into a training dataset, learn a visuomotor policy, and run it back on the real arm as a closed loop. Two policies share the pipeline — ACT (action-chunking transformer) and a flow-matching behaviour-cloning policy — both reimplemented in one PyTorch package with a single checkpoint format, so the deployment node loads one framework and switches policies by checkpoint alone.

The design rule throughout is that the data contract is the interface. Joint order, topic names, image sizes and sampling rates live in one YAML file (src/converter/config/so101.yaml), and every stage — recorder, converter, trainer, inference node — validates against it and errors rather than warns on drift. Contract violations that would otherwise be silent (a reordered joint, a resized image) fail loudly instead of training into a policy that deploys to nothing.

System

  • Control — ROS 2 Jazzy + ros2_control over Feetech STS3215 servos. A relay node mirrors the leader arm's joint states onto the follower's position commands; a mock hardware mode exercises the whole graph without the robot attached.
  • Bring-up — contract-derived launch for the controllers and the two cameras (wrist + third-person), including the Fast-DDS and usb_cam configuration that raw 480×640 image topics need to stream stably.
  • Recorder — episode recorder with keyboard start / stop / discard, writing rosbag2 MCAP and reporting per-topic rates after each save, so a bad episode is caught at collection time rather than at training time.
  • Converter — offline CLI turning rosbags into a LeRobot v3.0 dataset. Frames are built on a fixed 30 Hz grid by as-of sampling: each tick takes the newest sample older than that instant, and the tick is dropped if it exceeds that feature's max_age_s. An episode with too many interior gaps is skipped outright — LeRobot synthesizes time as frame_index / fps, so a dropped tick in the middle would be recorded as a normal interval and silently distort joint velocities.
  • Inference — one synchronous ROS 2 node that runs either an ACT or a flow-matching checkpoint, executing action chunks closed-loop with the matching denormalization.

Policies

  • ACT — a standalone action-chunking transformer: the policy predicts a chunk of future absolute joint targets from the two camera views plus proprioception, with its own trainer and a --init-from path for continued training.
  • Flow matching — a flow-matching behaviour-cloning policy over the same observation and action contract, sharing ACT's encoder conventions and checkpoint format so the two are directly comparable and interchangeable at deployment. Trained; not yet evaluated on hardware — the results below are ACT's alone.

Both consume the identical dataset and emit 6-DoF absolute joint position targets in the canonical order shoulder_pan, shoulder_lift, elbow_flex, wrist_flex, wrist_roll, gripper, which is the same order the URDF, the controllers and the dataset use.

Data & training

300 teleoperated pick-and-place demonstrations, two camera streams (wrist and third-person) plus proprioception, recorded at 30 Hz and converted to LeRobot v3.0. Because raw bags are large (~55 MB/s with two uncompressed 480×640 streams), conversion runs on the collecting machine and only the video-encoded dataset moves to the training machine.

Both policies were trained on that same dataset for 300k steps each — identical data, identical observation and action contract — so that when the flow-matching policy is evaluated on hardware it is compared under matched conditions, not at whatever step each happened to converge.

Results — deployed ACT policy

60 logged rollouts on the real arm in one session, all from the same ACT checkpoint at 30 Hz. A trial counts as completed when the held cube ends stacked on the base cube; first attempt is the stricter subset where that happened without a regrasp after a miss or a drop. Intervals are Wilson 95%.

The control loop held 29.7–29.9 Hz against a 30 Hz request across every rollout, with mean inference 3.4 ms. Three rollouts logged a command dropout of a second or more (an input went stale, 3.4 s at worst); excluding all three moves in-distribution completion from 74% to 74% — the failures are the policy's, not the pipeline's.

Flow matching is next. Its checkpoint is trained on the same 300 demonstrations for the same 300k steps, and the inference node already runs it by pointing at that checkpoint — what is missing is the hardware session. It will run the protocol above unchanged: the same placements in the same interleaved order, so the two policies are compared on identical setups rather than on whatever the table looked like that afternoon.

Where it fails

In distribution, most failures are not missed grasps — the policy reaches the cube, lifts it and puts it on the base, and the stack then slides off within a second of release. The cube is being let go slightly off-centre and still moving, which the success criterion (standing 3 s after release) is written to catch. It is a placement-precision problem at the end of the episode, not a perception or grasping one.

Out of distribution the failure is different in kind: the policy reaches confidently for where the cube would have been in the demonstrations. That is the honest signature of behaviour cloning on 300 demonstrations — it interpolates the demonstrated workspace and does not extrapolate past it.

Evaluation harness

The session above was driven by a harness in the repository: it generates a paired, interleaved schedule, launches the inference node per trial, captures the achieved control rate and inference latency automatically, and reports success rates with Wilson intervals plus a McNemar test between policies. It exists so the flow-matching session yields a directly comparable table rather than a second set of numbers gathered a different way.
