Overview

A team project on evaluation methods for dexterous robot manipulation and grasping. The Peg-in-Hole framework connects teleoperated demonstrations to policy training and real-robot insertion. My main contribution is the robot teleoperation interface and synchronized demonstration collection. The project progressed from position-controlled insertion shown at AutomationWorld 2026 to a policy with tactile observations.

My contribution — teleoperation & data collection

  • Wrote the robot-side control code: xArm6 + Robotiq Hand-E gripper driven by a SpaceMouse 6-DoF input device, so an operator could demonstrate the task by hand instead of scripting trajectories.
  • Mapped SpaceMouse twist input to end-effector motion with the gripper on the same loop, and streamed synchronized arm state, gripper state and RealSense frames as the demonstration record — a demonstration is only usable if those three arrive aligned.
  • Operated the rig to collect part of the demonstration dataset the policies trained on.

Team learning and deployment

The team trained Diffusion Policy and flow-matching policies and executed them on the real xArm6. My primary role in the original framework was teleoperation and data collection; the learning side was developed collaboratively within the team.

Team outcome

The framework ran live at AutomationWorld 2026 in March 2026. That version used position control without force or tactile sensing, and connector misalignment could block insertion. Repeated-trial insertion success rates are not published here.

AutomationWorld 2026 and insertion rollouts

XELA tactile observations for flow matching

XELA sensors on the gripper fingers measure the distribution of contact forces. These measurements were added to the flow-matching policy’s observations during imitation learning. This is a learned tactile-observation extension, not a separate explicit force-control or admittance loop.

The policy uses the contact information during insertion, where small alignment errors can prevent position-only execution. The recordings show contact distributions changing over the fingertips as insertion proceeds, alongside the robot’s motion.

The videos demonstrate execution. A controlled tactile/no-tactile comparison of success rate and insertion time has not yet been aggregated.
